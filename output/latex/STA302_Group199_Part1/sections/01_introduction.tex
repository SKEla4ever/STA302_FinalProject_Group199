\section{Introduction}

Airbnb prices can differ between weekday and weekend stays. Studying whether the price difference is similar for both options is significant for travellers comparing entire homes and private rooms. Our research question is: Across ten European cities, is the adjusted weekend--weekday difference in mean advertised prices the same for entire homes/apartments and private rooms? The response, realSum, is the total advertised price in euros for two guests staying two nights. The predictors of main focus are day type and room type, with adjustments for city, listing size, guest ratings, Superhost status, host portfolio size, and location.

Although raw group means may reflect differences in listing characteristics, multiple linear regression (MLR) better compares weekend and weekday prices within the same city while holding other measured characteristics fixed. We apply day type \(\times\) room type interaction to estimate, in euros, how much the adjusted weekend--weekday price difference for entire homes/apartments differs from that for private rooms. The model allows weekend--weekday differences to vary by city while estimating one common interaction across cities. We test whether this interaction is zero and report a confidence interval to show the uncertainty in the estimate. Moreover, residual diagnostics will check whether the model assumptions are reasonable.

Earlier studies support our predictors and design. \citet{gyodi2021}, who collected our data, found that listing size, quality, and location were all associated with Airbnb prices across these ten cities, supporting our choice of variables. In Bristol, \citet{voltes2020} found that models using property, host, and location characteristics fit entire-property prices better than private-room prices. This motivates checking whether the weekend--weekday difference also varies by room type. \citet{casamatta2022} used host type \(\times\) month interactions, and their OLS model found that listings from professional hosts had daily rates about 19\% higher than those from non-professional hosts in August, with a much smaller gap outside peak months in Corsica. Their model allowed the price gap between host types to vary by month. We use a similar approach to test whether the weekend--weekday price difference varies by room type, which extends the original ten-city study.

The findings can help travellers understand how advertised accommodation costs differed by day type and room type in the sampled cities, and give hosts a reference when setting weekend prices. However, since the weekday and weekend samples are unpaired, the estimates only describe associations in the sampled market.
