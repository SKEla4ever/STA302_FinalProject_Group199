\section{Ethics discussion}

Based on the ethics module's trustworthiness criteria, we consider the dataset suitable. The data were collected, not simulated, since \citet{gyodi2021} used automated Airbnb searches for two-person, two-night stays listing prices. Their peer-reviewed article describes the data source and collection process with a permanent DOI, and the Zenodo record documents the variables used in our analysis. The MD5 checksums of the 20 raw CSV files matched those in the archive, confirming that we downloaded the archived files intact.

However, the popularity of a dataset does not mean it is ethical to use. The researchers collected information from public Airbnb listings, so we do not know whether the hosts agreed to have their listings used for research. The dataset does not include host names or IDs, but the recorded coordinates could still help someone identify a property. To protect hosts' privacy, we report results for groups of listings and do not show individual locations.

The dataset was released under a CC BY 4.0 license, and we credit the authors and cite the Zenodo record. Therefore, we think this is an ethical way to use the data for the project.
